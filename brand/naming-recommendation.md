## Recommended: **Adsum**

**Domain to register: `adsumdesk.com`** — verified available (RDAP 404, whois `No match`).
Also grab `adsumintake.com`, `adsumline.com`, `adsumco.com` as defensive holds (all verified 404).

> **Adsum** — Latin, *"I am here."* The word you say when your name is called.

### What it signals

The product's promise is not intelligence. It is **presence** — something is there
at 11pm on a Saturday when the call comes in, and it behaves the same way every
time. "Adsum" says exactly that in one word, and it says it in a register that is
calm rather than clever. Nothing about it sounds like a growth hack. That matters
when the whole differentiator is guardrails: a name that sounds reckless
undermines the pitch before you open your mouth.

It also sits in the same etymological world as **Imbas** — Latin/mythic, a word
with a story behind it. The parent-and-child relationship reads as deliberate to
anyone who looks, without welding the two names together. That's the exact
relationship the brief asked for: a separate business, visibly of the same house.

### Why it survives the trades pivot

Because it has no content to contradict. "Adsum answers every call for your
roofing company, 24/7" is a sentence you can say without flinching. There is no
"intake", no "counsel", no professional-services coding to strip out. The pivot
costs you a headline and some screenshots — not a name.

Compare candidate #8, *First Call Intake*: a roofer does not have "intake," he has
leads. That name quietly bills you two weeks of repositioning the day you pivot.

### The spell test — this is why it wins

You told me the name gets said out loud on sales calls and typed from memory.
**Adsum** is five letters, fully phonetic, with exactly one plausible spelling.
No silent letters, no `ae`, no homophone. Say it once and it gets typed right.

Nothing else on the shortlist passes this cleanly. *Praesera* is the cleanest name
in the list on every other axis and it fails here alone — the `ae` is fatal, and
that single failure is disqualifying for a name that lives on phone calls.

### What it costs you — stated plainly

**1. You do not get `adsum.com`.** It is registered (RDAP 200). So is `adsum.ai`.
You launch on `adsumdesk.com`, which means one extra word on every verbal handoff
and a business card that isn't the cleanest possible object. This is the real price.

**2. The namespace is crowded.** At least three live software/AI companies already
use "Adsum": a dev shop in India, an AI unstructured-data platform serving
maritime/logistics, and a UK VAT-advance fintech doing roughly $8.7M. **None is in
AI intake, receptionist, or answering services**, and none is consumer-famous — a
solo attorney in Ohio has never heard of any of them. But it does mean that
someone who types bare "adsum" into Google lands somewhere that isn't you, and it
means your trademark clearance is a real conversation with a lawyer rather than a
formality. Budget for that conversation.

**3. It requires one sentence of explanation.** "It's Latin — it means *I am here*."
That sentence is an asset on a sales call, not a liability. But it is a sentence
you will say several thousand times.

### What I'd do on day one
Register `adsumdesk.com` plus the three defensive holds (~$50/yr total), check the
`@adsumdesk` handles by hand on X and Instagram — I could not verify these, see the
caveat in the candidates file — and put a trademark attorney on a Class 42/35
clearance before spending a dollar on design.

## Runner-up: **Custodeon** — `custodeon.com`, exact match, verified available

The only shortlist name where you own the exact-match `.com` outright *and* the
name is genuinely ownable. Coined from Latin *custos* (guardian, keeper), which
maps directly onto the compliance-and-guardrails story. The trademark screen came
back with zero exact hits — the cleanest availability position of any candidate.

**Why it isn't first:** in American English, *custodian* means the person who mops
the floor. Every prospect will hear "Custodian?" and you will correct them, on
every call, forever — and the association you're correcting *toward* is
janitorial. For a brand charging $12,000 to skeptical professionals, that's a
positioning wound that never fully closes. Nine letters to spell out doesn't help.

**Pick this if** owning the exact `.com` and having zero namespace competition
matters more to you than the janitor association. It's a defensible choice, not a
wrong one.

## Third: **Imbas Reception** — `imbasreception.com`, exact match, verified available

The whole `imbas*` namespace is open (`imbasdesk`, `imbasintake`, `imbasvigil`,
`imbasrelay`, `imbasanswer`, `imbassignal`, `imbaswatch`, `imbasconcierge`,
`imbasengine`, `imbasfrontdesk` — all verified 404). Cheapest, safest, fastest.
"Reception" is the single most niche-agnostic word available: a law firm has one,
a roofing company has one, and neither needs it explained.

**Two honest problems.**

First, it contradicts the brief. You said this is a sub-brand — *a separate
business with its own domain*. Prefixing it with the parent welds them together.
If the AI intake business needs to be sold, spun out, or positioned independently
of a software factory, you have made that structurally harder for the price of a
$12 domain you'd have saved anyway.

Second, and more immediately: **"Imbas" is the hard part of the name, and this
strategy doubles down on it.** It gets typed "Imbus", "Embas", "Imbass". The
parent already absorbs that cost; a new brand doesn't have to inherit it.

**Pick this if** the software factory's credibility is a material asset in the
sale — if "we're Imbas Solutions, we build software, this is our intake product"
opens doors that a standalone name wouldn't. That's a real strategic argument.
It's just a different strategy than the one the brief described.

## Names I rejected, and why

These are more useful than the shortlist.

**Anything containing law / legal / counsel / attorney / firm / case / esq** —
hard-filtered before I started. This killed a lot of genuinely good names. It was
the right call and you should hold the line on it: the entire thesis is that the
engine is 85% identical for a roofer, and a name is the one asset you cannot
refactor in two weeks.

**Praesera** (`praesera.com`, available, zero trademark hits) — the most
*brandable* name I found. Killed on one criterion: the `ae`. It cannot be typed
from memory, and you told me typing-from-memory is a requirement. A name that
fails its one hard requirement is not a near-miss, it's a reject.

**Exauda** (`exauda.com`, available) — beautiful root (Latin *exaudi*, to hear and
answer a call). Killed twice: the pronunciation is ambiguous ("ex-OW-da"?
"ex-AW-da"?), and **Exaud** is a real 14-year-old software-and-AI company in
Portugal. One letter apart, same industry. Not worth the fight.

**The entire descriptive category** — *Front Desk Engine*, *The Answering Engine*,
*First Response Desk*. Every one is available, every one spells perfectly, and I'd
still steer you away. "Frontdesk AI" (myaifrontdesk.com) and "FrontDesk"
(frontdesk.care) are both live AI-receptionist brands, and RingCentral, Synthflow
and Nextiva all market against the same phrase. Descriptive names in a crowded
category are close to unprotectable and they price you as a commodity. You are
charging $12,000 for the middle tier. Commodity naming and premium pricing fight
each other, and the pricing loses.

**Anything with "Intake" in it** (*First Call Intake*, *Guardrail Intake*,
*Imbas Intake*) — "intake" is clinical and legal-coded vocabulary. Roofers and
HVAC companies say *leads*. Putting the launch niche's jargon in the permanent
name is precisely the mistake the niche-agnostic constraint exists to prevent.

**"Guardrail" as a brand word** — it's the right *concept* and the wrong *word*.
"Guardrails" is AI-industry insider vocabulary. Your buyer is a skeptical
professional who has never read an alignment paper. Sell the guardrails in the
copy; don't put them in the name.

**Nightbell, Anteroom, Openline, Intakely, Answerly, Nevermiss, Casegate** — your
prior list. Confirmed dead, and "Casegate" would have failed the niche filter anyway.

**~250 other domains** — the real story of this exercise is that the short
brandable `.com` space is not picked over, it is *exhausted*. Single Latin words
(`adsum`, `vigil`, `custos`, `praesto`, `excubia`, `perennia`, `portico`, `limen`),
mythic names (`argos`, `vesta`, `bifrost`, `heimdal`, `pharos`), Irish roots
(`eolas`, `solas`, `fios`, `teine`, `failte`), two-word English compounds
(`everwatch`, `keepwatch`, `wardline`, `gatehouse`, `stillpoint`, `truepost`), and
even *invented* six-letter words (`kavora`, `noctara`, `orvia`, `sentiro`,
`vigilora`) all came back registered. `.ai` is equally gone: `adsum.ai`,
`imbas.ai`, `vigil.ai`, `portico.ai`, `cairn.ai`, `custos.ai` — all taken.

That is why the recommendation is a great root on a suffixed domain rather than a
perfect one-word `.com`. The perfect one-word `.com` does not exist at this price
point, and any list that claims otherwise hasn't checked.
